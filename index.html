      FAQ

      Questions about the product.

      The current demo runs in English with Amharic translation and voice output. The platform is designed to support any official language or regional lingua franca. Adding a new language requires translating the question set and recording or generating audio - a process that takes days, not months. The WHO IMCI protocol is the same regardless of language.
      All audio is pre-recorded and stored directly on the device. Nothing is streamed. The CHW hears each question aloud the moment they tap "Play" - regardless of whether they have any signal. This is a deliberate design choice: the tool must work identically in a city clinic and a village with no infrastructure.
      After the final question, the device gives one of three outputs. Green means manage at home - the CHW receives specific caregiver guidance to deliver. Yellow means monitor closely - the CHW schedules a follow-up within 24 hours and the case is logged. Red means refer immediately - the CHW is told to bring the child to a health facility without delay, and the supervisor is automatically notified via the dashboard on the next sync. TriageAid does not handle the referral itself - it flags it, logs it, and alerts the right people.
      The device follows WHO IMCI protocol exactly. If the output is wrong, the clinical logic of the protocol is wrong - that is a WHO accountability question, not ours. Our responsibility is faithful implementation, which is auditable because the decision tree logic is open. TriageAid is also not the final word - a red flag sends the child to a facility where a clinician makes the diagnosis. We make the first triage decision more reliable. We do not replace the care chain.
      The NGO holds regional spare devices. A broken device is swapped immediately. The CHW logs in with their 4-digit PIN on the replacement and their full case history restores from the cloud instantly - because all data lives on the platform, not the device. In the meantime, the CHW works as they do today. We improve on the baseline. We do not have to be perfect.
      Hardware - a ruggedized Android tablet and solar power bank - costs approximately $135 per CHW as a one-time deployment cost. The platform subscription is $8 per CHW per month, paid by the NGO or government program. This covers the supervisor dashboard, protocol updates, and support. There is no per-patient fee. A CHW conducting 10 triages a day or 1 generates the same cost.
